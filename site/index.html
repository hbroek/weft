Weft is a home for your posts, replies and likes. Your site lives at your own domain, everything you write sits in one file you can take with you, and anyone with a feed reader can follow you.

What Weft is

Weft is a small server for people who write. You post short notes or long articles in Markdown. Each post has an address that never changes, and your site is an ordinary RSS feed, so it works with the readers people already use. Weft adds what a feed lacks: replies, likes and conversations that reach across sites.

It grows out of textcasting, the idea that text should travel as freely as podcasts do, through plain feeds that nobody owns.

The ideas, in the words we use

The name comes from weaving. The weft is the thread that runs across and ties the lengthwise threads together. Posts, replies and likes are the threads that tie one person's site to another's.

Spool

Your spool is where your posts, replies, likes, follows and pictures are saved. It is one file, and it is yours: download it any time, back it up, or carry it to another server.

Loom (the server)

The loom is the server that hosts your site. It weaves spools into sites: it serves your pages and your feed, finds out when someone replies or likes, and fetches the feeds you follow.

Federation

There is no single Weft company or network. Anyone can run a loom (a Weft server), and looms talk to each other over open standards: RSS feeds to read, and Webmention to say "this replied to you" or "this liked your post". You can follow, reply to and like people on other looms, and on sites that are not Weft at all.

Portability

Because your spool is one file and your address is your own domain, leaving is easy. Download my data gives you everything. Move to another server carries your whole spool, your pictures included, to another loom (server), and leaves a redirect behind so the old address keeps working.

What you can do

Post

Write in Markdown, short or long. Add pictures by pasting or uploading; they are stored in your spool, so a post never breaks when someone else's server disappears.

Reply

Answer a post anywhere on the web. Your reply is a post on your own site that points at the one it answers, and the other side is told. Conversations show as threads.

Like

One plain signal: this was worth reading. No dislikes, no counts to chase. Every post has a feed of who liked it.

Repost

Share someone's post with your own words on top. The copy carries a canonical link back to the original, so readers can see where it came from, and the original stays the original.

Follow

Follow any feed, Weft or not, and read them together in one timeline.

Move

Change servers without losing your followers or your history, with a short lock instead of a long outage.

Three ways to start wefting

1. Your own domain

Your site lives at weft. plus a domain you own. It stays yours wherever you go.

2. The waiting list

No domain? Ask for an account at yourname.weftseed.com. Places are limited, and there is no guarantee that an account will be issued.

3. Your own server

Can't wait? Nothing stops you from running your own loom, today, and still following and replying to everyone.

1. With a domain of your own

If you can add DNS records to your domain, your site lives at weft. followed by it, such as weft.example.com or weft.example.co.uk, and it stays yours wherever you go. The name is always weft. plus your registered domain, never a name further down such as weft.blog.example.com.

  1. Give the domain you own. Your address is weft. followed by it, for example weft.example.com, and choose the account name you want.
  2. Add the DNS records we show you. A TXT record proves the domain is yours, and a CNAME points it at our loom (the server that hosts you).
  3. Choose a password. Your site is live at your own domain.

2. From the waiting list

No domain? You can still ask for an account. It lives at yourname.weftseed.com, on our loom (the server), and works like any other. When you get a domain later, you can add it from your profile. And if you ever want to leave, Move to another server carries your whole spool to the new place, and we redirect the old links so nothing you have shared breaks.

Ask for an account

Tell us the name you want and where you already write. Each request goes onto the list, and we read the list by hand.

No guarantees. The number of accounts is limited, and joining the list does not guarantee that an account will be issued, or an answer by any date. We let people in from the list by hand, and we may never get to your request. An account with a domain keeps working as long as the domain reaches us; if it stops, we warn you and give you time to fix it or to move away with your data. Questions? Write to the operator (turn on JavaScript to see the address).

3. Run your own server

This is the way that needs nobody's permission, and you can begin right now. Weft is one program with no outside services to set up. Start it (you are now running a loom), point a wildcard DNS name at it, and add people with weft user add. Your server and ours can follow, reply to and like each other. The program and its documentation are on GitHub, and the feed extensions are described at weftprotocol.org.

Questions

Why a domain? Spam. A domain costs a little and cannot be made by the thousand. Why a file? So the data is yours. Can I leave? Yes, at any time, with everything.
